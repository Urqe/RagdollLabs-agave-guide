# Editorial voice guide

Status: adopted 2026-10-24 (Pair 23.1). Every new or rewritten reader-facing text, in English and Spanish, follows this guide. Pairs 24–29 write against it; Pair 28 audits the older surfaces against it.

## Who is talking

Someone who knows this spirit well and likes it, talking to a curious friend across the table. They are proud of where it comes from and want you to enjoy it.

They are not a character. There is no invented "I", no childhood memory, no grandmother and no invented quote. The warmth comes from how the text talks (direct, concrete, glad to share), not from a made-up biography.

## The order of a profile

1. **The glass first.** What you will likely smell and taste, how it feels and how it finishes. Use everyday words.
2. **Why it might taste that way.** Plant, place and method, stated as a likely reason, never as proof.
3. **What sets it apart** from the style next to it.
4. **The example bottle**, then the sources.

Legal, composition and safety facts stay in the sentence where they matter. Everything about method, reviewer provenance and how the guide decides stays in the sources and "how this guide works" disclosures, not in the opening paragraph.

## Caution, said once

Batches vary, tendencies are not rules, and one bottle is not a whole state. Say that **once per profile**, plainly, where it applies, and then move on. Don't stack three disclaimers in one paragraph and don't end every paragraph with one.

- Good: "That's one family's mezcal; other Guerrero lots can pour hotter."
- Too much: "Those are readings of this vinata's lots. Other mezcals share the state's tools, not one flavour: batches genuinely vary, and a different producer may read quite different."

When a cause is unproven, say what is known and what is not in one or two plain sentences: "Several tasters put that creamy tang down to the pulque that starts the fermentation. It's a fair guess; nobody has tested it."

## Regional voice means specifics, not costume

A Jalisco page sounds like Jalisco because it uses Jalisco's words and places, not because it puts on an accent. Name the local plant name, the town, the maker and the tool: vinata, tahona, Los Altos, palenque, maestra mezcalera, horno, penca ancha. Explain a local word the first time it appears, in a few words.

Pride is welcome when it's earned and concrete ("a distinction the valley is proud of"). Skip claims nobody can back up ("the taste of the land itself", "the soul of Mexico") and skip superlatives.

## Sentences

- Short sentences, active voice, concrete nouns.
- Anchor flavours to things people know: green apple, olive brine, campfire, buttered toast.
- Attribution is light: "tasters find", "in reviews of the example bottle". Use it once in a paragraph, never as the opening of every sentence.
- Don't open with the guide or the record: no "This profile is grounded in…", "This guide records…", "What the glass shows and how it is made are separate records".
- Don't narrate the method of the guide inside tasting text.

### Words to drop or replace

| Avoid | Use instead |
| --- | --- |
| documented method / documented tasting | how it's made / what tasters find |
| reads as, reads savory | tastes, comes across as, leans |
| genuinely (as an intensifier) | delete it |
| it is not a mechanism the sources establish | nobody has shown that; it's a guess |
| a tendency, not a rule (repeated) | say it once, or "often", "usually" |
| welcome (for the first impression) | first sip, nose, opening |
| one bottle's welcome is not the state's | that's one bottle, not all of Guerrero |

## Spelling and labels

- English edition: British spelling, matching the UK flag (flavour, colour, savoury). Pair 28 aligns the existing mix.
- Home-page paths: **Start here** (the five-step journey, `#/start`), **Explore** (styles, flavours, plants and places), **Look it up** (catalogue, profiles, glossary). The nav item **Learn** stays the reference chapters. "Learn" is not used as the name of the beginner path.
- Sensory scale words appear once per dimension. Body is shown as "Light / Medium / Full body", not also as "Low".

## Spanish (Mexico)

Write it, don't translate it. A Mexican reader should not be able to tell which language came first.

- Use tú in lessons and UI.
- Prefer natural tasting verbs: sabe a, huele a, se siente, trae, deja. Avoid "se lee" and "leerse" for flavour.
- Watch false friends: English "savory" is **not** "sabroso" (which means tasty). Use "salado", "más vegetal que dulce", "terroso", "con un fondo salino", depending on the note.
- No English loanwords where Mexican Spanish has a word: "funky" → "matiz fermentado", "taffy" → "caramelo masticable".
- Keep legal terms exact (Denominación de Origen, Indicación Geográfica, NOM) and proper names unchanged.
- Known calques to avoid: "genuinamente divididas", "el agave firma", "carga la presión de sostenibilidad", "una bienvenida" for a first sip.

## Before and after

Guerrero papalote, the opening of the description.

**Before:**
> This profile is grounded in one bottle: the example expression, made by the Alcaraz family in Apango, whose reviewers find a fruit-salad nose (grape, apple, banana) with mint and green pepper…

**After (EN):**
> Fruit comes first here, not smoke. The example bottle, from the Alcaraz family in Apango, opens with a fruit-salad nose (grape, apple, banana), a breath of mint and green pepper, then a juicy, easy palate and a warm finish with a touch of vanilla.

**After (ES):**
> Aquí la fruta llega antes que el humo. La botella de ejemplo, de la familia Alcaraz en Apango, abre con una nariz de ensalada de frutas (uva, manzana, plátano), un toque de menta y pimiento verde, luego un trago jugoso y fácil y un final cálido con algo de vainilla.

## Checklist for every rewrite

- [ ] The first sentence is about the glass, not the guide.
- [ ] A specific person, place or local word appears.
- [ ] Uncertainty is stated once, where it applies.
- [ ] No fact was added or dropped. Legal and composition precision is intact.
- [ ] The Spanish was written for a Mexican reader, not mapped word by word.
